Lesson 2.12 Bet

Analyze AI agent behavior in your logs

Written by Michael Ofei Reviewed by Kevin Indig Updated May 20, 2026 Read time ~4 minutes

Server log analysis is old news for traditional crawlers — SEOs have mined logs for years to see what Googlebot fetches. The bet here is that the same discipline, applied to AI agents, reveals something new: which of your pages AI systems actually retrieve in real time, and how often. Because AI agents fetch and validate content at query time, log analysis can show you what's being used, not just what's indexed.

Why it might work

Traditional crawl logs tell you what got indexed. AI agent logs could tell you what's getting retrieved and cited right now — a more direct signal of what's actually working in AI search.

If you can see that GPTBot fetches your comparison pages ten times more often than your blog posts, that's a real input to where you invest. The mechanism is sound: AI agents do hit your server, and those requests are logged. The open question is what you do with the data once you have it.

What we've seen

This one is early. Pulling AI user-agents out of your logs is technically straightforward — the requests are right there — but the playbook for acting on the data isn't established yet.

When Kevin Indig reviewed this for the course, he named the gap directly: it's a helpful analysis, but it's not yet clear what you'd do differently on a page that agents don't retrieve, or how that action would differ from the tactics already covered. That's the honest state of this Bet — the data is available, the resulting actions are still being figured out. We're including it because the signal is promising and the cost to start watching is low, not because the playbook is proven.

How to try it

  • Filter your server logs for AI user-agents (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, and others).
  • Track retrieval frequency by page and by bot over time — look for which content gets fetched, and which never does.
  • Cross-reference with your citation tracking: do the pages agents retrieve correlate with the pages that get cited?
  • Treat findings as a hypothesis generator, not a directive — surface patterns worth testing, then test them.

Watch out for

  • The open question, plainly: it's not yet clear what action you take on a page agents don't retrieve, or whether it differs from your other tactics. If you can't answer that for your own site, the analysis is interesting but not yet actionable.
  • Don't over-invest before the playbook exists. This is a watch-and-learn Bet — collect the data and look for patterns, but don't restructure your strategy around it yet.
References
