Lesson 2.10 Best Practice

Conduct prompt research

Written by Michael Ofei Reviewed by Kevin Indig Updated May 20, 2026 Read time ~5 minutes

Keyword research tells you what people type into a search box. Prompt research tells you what they ask an AI assistant — longer, more specific, more conversational questions. Conducting prompt research means identifying the high-intent prompts that matter to your business, then building content keyed to those specific questions rather than broad keywords.

Why it works

AI users don't search the way Google users do. Instead of "best leggings," a shopper asks "what are the best squat-proof leggings for hot yoga that don't roll down at the waist, under $100?" The specificity changes everything — for a DTC brand like Gymshark, the page that answers that exact question wins, not the one optimized for the broad category.

Traditional keyword
Broad — high volume, low intent

"best leggings"

AI prompt
Specific — the question AI actually answers

"What are the best squat-proof leggings for hot yoga that don't roll down at the waist, under $100?"

This is content strategy, not content structure. Passage-level optimization is about how you write; prompt research is about what you write about. It sits upstream of the rest of the content work — it tells you which questions are worth answering in the first place. The unit of optimization is shifting from the keyword to the question1 2 — AI users ask in full sentences, and the content that earns the citation is the one that answers the specific question.

How to apply

  • Mine real prompts: customer interviews, sales-call questions, support tickets, and your own AI assistant logs.
  • Cluster prompts by intent — comparison, recommendation, troubleshooting, validation.
  • Map prompts to content: which existing pages answer them, and which gaps need new content.
  • Write to the specific prompt, not the broad keyword. Match the question's specificity in your answer.
  • Feed the priority prompts into your content-formats work (the next lesson) so the formats answer real questions.

Watch out for

  • Don't abandon keyword research. Traditional search still drives real volume — prompt research complements it, it doesn't replace it.
  • Prompts shift faster than keywords as a category's AI usage matures. Re-run prompt research periodically rather than treating it as a one-time exercise.
