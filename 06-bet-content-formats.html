Lesson 2.11 Bet

Publish in AI-extractable content formats

Written by Michael Ofei Reviewed by Kevin Indig Updated May 20, 2026 Read time ~5 minutes

Some content formats get surfaced by AI systems more readily than others — comparison tables, "X vs Y" pages, "best for [audience]" guides, FAQ structures, decision-tree content. The idea behind this Bet: deliberately produce more of the formats AI tends to lift and cite, as a portfolio decision about which content types you invest in. It's distinct from passage-level optimization (how you structure any given page) — this is about which kinds of pages you choose to build in the first place.

Why it might work

AI assistants frequently answer comparison and recommendation queries — "best X for Y," "X vs Y," "alternatives to X." Content explicitly structured to answer those question shapes is easier for a model to extract and present as a direct answer.

A comparison table, for instance, maps cleanly onto the structured response an AI assistant wants to generate. The bet is that matching your content formats to the question shapes AI users actually ask increases your odds of being the source it pulls from.

Comparison tables
"Leggings A vs B vs C, side by side"
"X vs Y" pages
"Gymshark vs Lululemon for squats"
"Best for [audience]" guides
"Best leggings for hot yoga, for tall frames, under $100"
FAQ structures
"Are they squat-proof? Do they shrink in the wash?"
Decision-tree content
"Find your fit in three questions"

What we've seen

Different content formats get surfaced at different rates. Comparison and recommendation formats map cleanly onto what AI assistants generate, so they tend to get lifted more often than plain prose.1 The signal is real but early — we don't yet have strong causal evidence that producing more of these formats lifts citation share predictably.

That's why this is a Bet rather than a Best Practice: the mechanism is sound and the cost is mostly editorial, but the research isn't conclusive yet. Treat it as a directional experiment, not a proven play.

How to try it

  • Identify the comparison, alternative, and "best for" queries that matter in your category. (Prompt research, the previous Best Practice, feeds directly into this.)
  • Pick 3-5 high-intent question shapes and build dedicated pages for them — comparison tables, decision guides, FAQ structures.
  • Structure each page so the answer is extractable: clear headers, tabular data where it fits, self-contained sections.
  • Track whether these formats earn citations over 60-90 days before scaling the investment.

Watch out for

  • This is a portfolio bet, not a guarantee. Some formats will land, others won't — treat the first batch as an experiment and let citation data guide where you double down.
  • Don't manufacture comparison or "best for" pages where you have no genuine authority. Thin, formulaic format-chasing reads as low-quality to both readers and AI systems.
