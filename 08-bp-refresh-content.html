Lesson 2.9 Best Practice

Refresh high-authority content regularly

Written by Michael Ofei Reviewed by Kevin Indig Updated May 20, 2026 Read time ~4 minutes

AI systems weight recent, accurate content more heavily — and they'll happily surface outdated information about your brand if that's what's freshest in their reach. Refreshing high-authority content means keeping your most-cited, most-visible pages current, so AI systems retrieve an accurate picture of your brand rather than a stale one.

Why it works

Outdated pages don't just underperform — they actively mislead. If your most-cited page still describes a product tier you discontinued, AI assistants will repeat it to anyone who asks. Freshness signals — visible publish and updated dates, current data, recent examples — help AI systems prefer your current content over older cached versions.1

This compounds with the rest of the Discoverability work. A page that's technically reachable, well-structured, and current is far more likely to be retrieved and cited accurately than one that's only two of the three. Industry guidance on preparing content for AI agents points to freshness as a recurring factor in what gets surfaced.2

How to apply

  • Identify your highest-authority, most-cited pages. (The content audit in Lesson 2.5 produces this list.)
  • Audit them for stale information: discontinued products, old statistics, outdated claims, dead examples.
  • Update the substance and the freshness signals together — current data plus accurate publish/updated dates.
  • Prioritize by visibility × staleness: fix the most-seen, most-outdated pages first.
  • Set a refresh cadence for your cornerstone pages so it doesn't depend on someone remembering.

Watch out for

  • Don't refresh for the sake of changing a date. AI systems and readers both reward substantive updates, not cosmetic timestamp changes.
  • Freshness matters most for time-sensitive content. Evergreen frameworks and definitions may not need frequent updates — spend the effort where staleness actually misleads.
